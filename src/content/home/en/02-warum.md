## The hard moments happen between appointments.

Around 5 to 7 % of school-age children have ADHD, which often means one or two children in every
class. Even more children struggle with individual symptoms.

Therapy usually takes place once a week. Medication helps many children, but not all. The time in
between often goes without support: the morning routine, packing the school bag, homework at four
o'clock. Parents become the "external reminder", and that is exactly where stress and conflict
arise.

**Our mission:** Children with attention difficulties should get support when they need it, in
everyday life and at school. To do this, we connect families, schools, therapy and research, and
develop digital tools that are evaluated scientifically.

<details>
<summary>Understanding ADHD</summary>

Many children with ADHD know exactly what to do. What is hard is getting started, sticking with it
and switching between tasks. ADHD is a neurobiological difference in development and has nothing
to do with intelligence or a lack of willpower.

- **Attention:** thoughts drift, getting started is hard, things get forgotten.
- **Self-regulation:** transitions are difficult, feelings change quickly, inner restlessness.
- **Structure:** routines need a lot of guidance, the sense of time is weak.

What works is outside structure, short clear prompts and positive feedback, ideally at the moment
they are needed. Parent counselling, occupational therapy, support at school and, where a doctor
recommends it, medication complement each other.
