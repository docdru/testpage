## The problem we are addressing

ADHD affects roughly 5–7% of school-age children. Studies and practical experience show
that the core challenges often lie not in cognitive understanding, but in
**self-regulation and attention control in everyday life**, especially in starting tasks,
staying on them over time, and transitioning between activities.

Medication, therapy and school support measures are important pillars. At the same time
they often act only at single points in time: therapy is usually once a week, school
support is situational, and medication does not help all children equally or comes with
side effects.

The qualitative studies in the ZHAW project made it clear that a gap opens up precisely in
**everyday learning and working**, at home or at school: children need subtle, situational
cues to build focus, maintain it and regulate themselves.

Writing is a key skill for school and personal development, but it is a particular
challenge for children with ADHD. Their reduced attention and fine-motor limitations mean
they can often only complete a fraction of the required writing work.

**FocusWatch targets exactly this gap.**
